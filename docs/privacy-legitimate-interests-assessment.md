# Legitimate Interests Assessment: Public Planning Data

Date: 20 August 2026

This is an internal operational record for OpenList. It records the assessment supporting the use of legitimate interests for the processing described below; it is not external legal advice.

## Scope

This assessment concerns OpenList's organisation and public display of core metadata from official public planning records. It does not authorise ingestion or publication of every field available in a source document.

## A. Purpose test

OpenList pursues legitimate interests in:

- improving discovery and usability of public planning information;
- enabling property and planning research;
- allowing users to search and compare planning activity;
- enabling monitoring around locations or planning applications where such functionality is introduced; and
- organising fragmented public data into a more usable form.

The broader benefit is making official information practically accessible, searchable and understandable in the property and planning context. The purpose is property and planning research, not profiling individuals.

## B. Necessity test

Planning references, address or location, applicant name where supplied by the source, proposal description, dates, status and decision may be necessary or useful to accurately identify, distinguish and understand a planning record. Reference numbers, locations and descriptions help prevent records being confused; dates, status and decision explain their lifecycle and current context.

The purpose cannot reasonably be achieved as effectively with only reference numbers or descriptions. However, applicant names are more privacy-sensitive than reference numbers or development descriptions. OpenList should minimise fields, should not ingest or expose additional personal information merely because it appears in source documents, and should regularly consider whether an applicant-name field remains necessary for accurate understanding of the particular record.

## C. Balancing test

### Nature of the data

The data is mostly ordinary personal data contained in official planning records. OpenList does not intentionally process special-category data through this activity.

### Source

The source is official public planning records and datasets.

### Reasonable expectations

People submitting planning applications can reasonably expect core application information to become part of a public planning process. That said, republication through a search-engine-friendly third-party service may make the information easier to discover than on the original authority site. That increased discoverability is a real consideration in the balancing exercise and must not be minimised.

### Impact

Potential risks include easier discovery of an individual's association with a property, search-engine indexing, outdated information remaining easy to find, confusion where source data is wrong, and particular circumstances in which publication creates a heightened privacy impact.

### Safeguards

- Process principally official planning metadata.
- Avoid unnecessary sensitive information.
- Identify the original public source.
- Maintain a clear public Privacy Notice and a privacy contact at privacy@openlist.ie.
- Provide correction and objection handling, and assess individual objections.
- Correct OpenList ingestion or display errors.
- Distinguish OpenList from the authoritative public record.
- Consider suppression or de-indexing where justified by a particular case.
- Do not use planning applicant information for unrelated marketing or individual profiling.

## D. Conclusion

OpenList can rely on legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR for the described processing of public planning metadata, provided the safeguards above remain in place, minimisation continues to be applied, and individual objections or heightened risks are assessed on their facts. This conclusion should be revisited if the data, publication model, source coverage or user-facing functionality materially changes.

## Article 14 position

Data comes primarily from official public sources. The scale of the dataset makes individual notification impractical, OpenList generally lacks independent contact details for people named in records, and direct notices could require substantial additional processing of personal data merely to deliver a notice. OpenList instead provides prominent public transparency information and a privacy contact. Article 14(5)(b) may apply where its conditions are met, but its applicability should be reassessed if the nature or scale of processing materially changes; Article 14 is not treated as never applying.
